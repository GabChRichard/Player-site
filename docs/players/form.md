## At the table: Offer, Complication, Want

Every character brings three things to the crew:

- an **Offer**: something useful they bring to the crew and the table;
- a **Complication**: something that can make their life harder, and the crew's with it;
- a **Want**: something they genuinely want, enough to take risks for it.

These are what the GM builds scenes from. Your Offer earns you the spotlight, your Complication brings trouble to the table, and your Want gives you a reason to make hard choices. Nobody's personal story has to be written in advance: it grows out of these three, session by session.

Make them **concrete**: a name, a place, a thing. "Contacts" is a category. "Ilsa Marr, who runs a back-alley med-bay on Absalom Station and owes me her life" is an Offer.

### Offer: what you bring to the crew

Something the crew can use that isn't on your character sheet. Not a class feature or a skill bonus, but access, knowledge or people that open doors your class can't.

Pick **one** assets from the table, or invent your own.

| Asset | Description | Gameplay opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| **Ally** | Someone willing to help you. | Assistance, missions, rescue, information. |
| **Contact** | Someone with access to a particular community or organization. | Introductions, negotiations, favors. |
| **Haven** | A safe place you can access. | Rest, repairs, hiding, resupply. |
| **Reputation** | People know your name or deeds. | Recognition, opportunities, intimidation, expectations. |
| **Legacy** | You inherited something meaningful. | Ancient technology, property, a title, a mysterious object. |

- **Asset type:**
- **What it is:**
- **How the crew can use it:**
- **Its limit:** what it can't do, how often it works, or what it costs to use.
- **Can the other players know about it?** Yes / no / later

### Complication: what makes your life harder

Something the GM can bring in to make a scene harder, more personal or more expensive. It isn't a flaw that makes your character useless. It's a problem that keeps coming back.

Pick **three** complications from the table, or invent your own.

| Complication | Description | Gameplay opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| **Debt** | You owe money, resources or favors. | Collection, negotiation, difficult choices. |
| **Bounty** | Someone wants you captured or dead. | Hunters, ambushes, false identities. |
| **Duty** | You have a responsibility you cannot easily abandon. | Rescue missions, moral dilemmas, urgent requests. |
| **Rival** | Someone actively competes against you. | Competition, sabotage, recurring encounters. |
| **Enemy** | Someone wants to hurt you. | Revenge, assassination, escalation. |
| **Secret** | Something about you must remain hidden. | Exposure, blackmail, revelations. |
| **Obligation** | Someone depends on you. | Family troubles, protection, difficult sacrifices. |
| **Infamy** | You have a bad reputation. | Distrust, discrimination, unwanted attention. |
| **Curse** | Something unusual affects your life. | Strange events, investigation, potential solutions. |
| **Oath** | You made a promise you must uphold. | Conflicting priorities, tests of character. |
| **Wanted** | An organization or government is looking for you. | Checkpoints, investigations, pursuit. |
| **Unfinished business** | Something important remains unresolved. | Returning locations, old missions, lost people. |

Every sentenced character already has a balance to pay. A **Debt** complication is something on top of it: a debt off the books.

- **Complication type:**
- **What it is:**
- **How it shows up at the table:** a person who arrives, a message, a demand, a reputation that walks in before you.
- **What happens if you ignore it:**
- **Can the other players know about it?** Yes / no / later

Complications always respect your [hard and soft limits](#hard-limits).

### Want: what you genuinely want

Not what the mission needs: what *your character* wants. Pick a **main drive** from the table, and a second one if they pull in different directions. The GM uses your drives to put opportunities in front of you, without forcing a storyline on you.

| Drive | Character motivation | Possible adventure |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | You want to understand the unknown. | Exploration, ruins, anomalies. |
| **Redemption** | You want to make up for past mistakes. | Difficult choices, second chances. |
| **Ambition** | You want power, status or recognition. | Politics, competition, leadership. |
| **Protection** | You want to protect someone or something. | Defense, rescue, sacrifice. |
| **Freedom** | You want to live without control. | Escaping authorities, liberation. |
| **Knowledge** | You seek forbidden or forgotten truths. | Research, conspiracies, mysteries. |
| **Revenge** | You want someone to pay. | Pursuit, confrontation, moral conflict. |
| **Belonging** | You want to find a place among others. | Community, relationships, loyalty. |
| **Wealth** | You want to become rich or independent. | Salvage, contracts, risky deals. |
| **Legacy** | You want to leave something behind. | Building, protecting, creating. |

- **Main drive:**
- **Second drive (optional):**

Then make it specific:

- **Right now:** what gets them out of their bunk? *Find someone who disappeared. Earn enough credits to buy a ship. Discover an ancient civilization. Become a renowned explorer. Escape someone who is hunting them.*
- **Over the campaign:** what would make them feel their life has meant something?
- **The impossible one:** a goal they know they'll never reach, and would still try for.
- **How they'd know they got it:** what success looks like, in one sentence.

Then the questions that test it:

- **What would tempt them to abandon the crew's mission?** A person, an opportunity, a discovery, a reward or a personal crisis.
- **What would they never willingly sacrifice?** A belief, a person, their freedom, their reputation or something else.
- **What would they give up to get what they want?**
